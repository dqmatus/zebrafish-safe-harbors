## A genome-wide, evidence-integrated catalog of zebrafish genomic safe-harbor sites

**Overview.** To enable single-copy, reliably-expressed transgene integration in zebrafish, we generated a genome-wide catalog of candidate **genomic safe-harbor (GSH) landing sites** — genomic positions that are constitutively accessible yet functionally inert. The approach integrates only **publicly available functional-genomics data** (no new sequencing) and defines safe harbors as regions that are (i) open across all of development and all cell types, (ii) distant from genes and their regulatory elements, and (iii) free of conserved, repetitive, or low-mappability sequence — then validates the catalog against known integration sites and outputs ready-to-use knock-in reagents for the top candidates. All coordinates are on GRCz11 (danRer11).

**Data foundation.** Candidate accessibility is called from **DANIO-CODE ATAC-seq** (31 samples spanning 11 developmental stages, unfertilized egg through 48 hpf); pre-zygotic-genome-activation stages are deliberately excluded, since a constitutive landing site need not be accessible before transcription begins. Inertness is enforced using Ensembl GRCz11 gene/exon/repeat annotations, a curated set of validated enhancers, promoters, and novel lncRNAs (Baranasic et al. 2022), sequence-conservation tracks (GERP constrained elements and Ancora conserved non-coding elements), genome-mappability masks, and **DANIO-CODE ChIP-seq** for regulatory marks (74 samples across seven laboratories; H3K27ac, H3K4me1, H3K4me3, H3K27me3, CTCF, plus H3K36me3, H2A.Z, H3K14ac, and Pol II). An orthogonal single-cell axis is provided by the **Zebrahub single-nucleus multiome snATAC atlas** (Bhattacharya et al. 2024).

**Constitutive accessibility.** Per-sample ATAC peak scores are quantile-normalized and median-collapsed hierarchically (replicate → biosample → developmental stage) to prevent any single over-sampled stage or lab from dominating. Fixed windows around stage-level summits are merged, and each region is scored by the number of stages in which it is accessible; we retain the strictest tier — regions open in **100% of stages (11/11)** — yielding **32,601 constitutively accessible windows** that are open across the entire developmental time course rather than transiently.

**Inertness and safety filtering.** Constitutive peaks are then filtered to remove any region that is not functionally silent. Regions are excluded if they fall within gene bodies (±10 kb), exons, essential genes (±20 kb), transposable elements (all major classes), telomere-proximal sequence, or non-primary scaffolds. Surviving candidates pass a **discard-whole regulatory screen**: any candidate overlapping an active or poised chromatin mark, validated enhancer, promoter, novel lncRNA, Polycomb domain, conserved non-coding element, or low-mappability/segmental-duplication region is dropped entirely. We additionally apply **GSH-style distance criteria adapted from mammalian safe-harbor standards** (minimum distances from cancer-associated and dosage-sensitive genes, microRNAs/ncRNAs, and ultraconserved elements). Notably, early developmental **pioneer transcription factors were evaluated and deliberately *not* used as a regulatory mask**, because they bind the genome promiscuously (e.g., Pou5f3 occupies ~3.8% of the genome) and would spuriously exclude genuinely inert sites. This screen yields a final catalog of high-confidence candidates.

**Annotation, ranking, and cross-validation.** Each candidate is annotated with its nearest gene and distance, size, GC content, repeat and conservation content, topological (TAD) context, and developmental stage count, and assigned a transparent composite rank that rewards constitutive breadth, region size, and gene distance while penalizing residual conserved or repetitive overlap. As an independent, orthogonal check, candidates are cross-referenced against **DANIO-CODE COPE elements — constitutively open chromatin that carries an ATAC-seq signal but *lacks* the active-enhancer/promoter histone marks of a functional cis-regulatory element ("open but inert")** — which corroborates the safe-harbor profile from a second, published annotation (the developmentally-regulated DOPE set is its dynamic counterpart). Because bulk ATAC establishes accessibility across developmental *stages* but not *cell types*, we additionally intersect the catalog with the Zebrahub single-nucleus atlas to confirm accessibility across cell identities.

**Validation.** The pipeline is benchmarked against experimentally validated integration sites (pIGLET landing sites and Sleeping Beauty founder loci). Because the best-characterized validated sites are gene-proximal — and therefore intentionally excluded by the strict distance criteria — we treat them as sensitivity checks rather than positive controls, and additionally emit a **relaxed-buffer tier** (2 kb gene distance) and an **intron-permissive tier** that surface gene-proximal-but-inert sites in the spirit of those benchmarks.

**Downstream reagent design.** For top candidates the pipeline enumerates SpCas9 (NGG) guide RNAs, scores them for genomic specificity and efficiency (CRISPOR: MIT/CFD + Doench '16), and designs complete knock-in reagents — PCR/HDR donors with homology arms and an integration cassette (loxP, FRT, and Bxb1 attP recombinase sites), genotyping and junction primers, and annotated wild-type and integrated sequence maps — so nominated sites are immediately actionable.
